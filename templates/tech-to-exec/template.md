# Template: Tech → Executive (SCQA, 5-7 slides)

## When to use
Your audience: VP, C-suite, Director. They don't know your tech. They care about business outcomes, risk, and time.

## Slide structure

---

### Slide 1 — Situation
**Purpose:** Establish shared context. Get them nodding.
**Pattern:** "We are [doing X] in [context Y]."
**Rule:** Only facts they already agree with. No new information here.
**Max words on slide:** 20

---

### Slide 2 — Complication
**Purpose:** Name the thing that changed or is at risk. Create tension.
**Pattern:** "But [something changed / is breaking / is at risk], which means [consequence]."
**Rule:** This is where you earn their attention. Make the consequence real and quantified.
**Hook options:** a dollar number, a percentage lost, a competitor action, a deadline

---

### Slide 3 — Answer (skip the "Question" slide for exec audiences)
**Purpose:** Your recommendation. Lead with it — don't make them wait.
**Pattern:** "We recommend [action] because it [business outcome]."
**Rule:** One recommendation per slide. If you have three options, present the one you believe in.

---

### Slide 4 — Evidence (repeat for each key point, max 2-3 evidence slides)
**Purpose:** Back up the recommendation.
**Pattern:** "[Data/example/case] shows [insight]."
**Rule:** One point per evidence slide. Not a list.

---

### Slide 5 — Risk / Objection Handler (optional)
**Purpose:** Address the most likely pushback before they raise it.
**Pattern:** "You might ask [objection]. Here's why that's manageable: [answer]."
**Rule:** Use only if you know there's a strong objection. Otherwise skip.

---

### Slide 6 — Ask
**Purpose:** Tell them exactly what you need.
**Pattern:** "We need [specific thing] by [date] to [achieve outcome]."
**Rule:** One ask. Make it easy to say yes.

---

## Speaker note template (per slide)

```
[Opening — 1 sentence that bridges from previous slide]
[Main point — what you say, not what the slide reads]
[Proof point or story — 1 concrete example]
[Bridge — why this leads to the next slide]
```

## Anti-patterns to avoid
- ❌ "Let me give you some background..." (they know the background, start with the problem)
- ❌ "Our architecture uses..." (they don't care)
- ❌ "In conclusion..." (if you need to say this, your deck failed)
- ❌ "Any questions?" as the last slide (replace with your ask)
- ❌ Agenda slide (wastes the opening)
