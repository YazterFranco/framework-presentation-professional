# Template: Tech → Investor (AIDA, 5 slides)

## When to use
Your audience: VC partner, angel investor, accelerator judge. They evaluate dozens of
pitches. They don't need context — they need to be grabbed in 10 seconds, then pulled
into a market they want to own.

## Slide structure

---

### Slide 1 — Attention
**Purpose:** One fact that reframes how big this problem is. Make them stop scrolling.
**Pattern:** "[Striking number or consequence] — and [who suffers from it]."
**Rule:** The number must be verifiable and sourced. Vague claims ("billions lost") lose credibility.
**Hook options:** cost of the problem, scale of underserved market, rate of failure
**Max words on slide:** 15 (the number does the work)

---

### Slide 2 — Interest
**Purpose:** Make the problem personal. They must feel it, not just understand it.
**Pattern:** A 2-sentence story of a specific person encountering the problem.
**Rule:** Use a real customer if you have one. Name and detail make it credible.
**What to avoid:** Generic personas ("imagine a small business owner..."). Use "Maria" not "a user".

---

### Slide 3 — Desire (What you built + traction)
**Purpose:** Show the solution AND the proof it works. Investors buy traction, not ideas.
**Pattern:** "[Product] does [specific action] in [timeframe] — [N customers paying $X/month]."
**Rule:** Lead with the outcome, not the mechanism. MRR beats architecture diagrams.
**Must include:** At least one traction metric (MRR, customers, NPS, retention rate, pilot commitments).

---

### Slide 4 — Desire (Why you win)
**Purpose:** Explain defensibility. Why can't a big company copy this tomorrow?
**Pattern:** "[Incumbent blind spot] + [your specific advantage] + [switching cost or moat]."
**Rule:** Name real competitors. Saying "there's no competition" kills credibility.
**Moat options:** proprietary data, switching cost, network effects, regulatory barrier, speed advantage

---

### Slide 5 — Action
**Purpose:** One specific ask with context. Make it easy to say yes in this meeting.
**Pattern:** "We're raising $[X] at [terms] — [% committed] — looking for [specific thing]."
**Rule:** Name the use of funds in one line. Name the lead ask explicitly.
**Never:** "We'd love your support / feedback / thoughts." Name a decision.

---

## Speaker note template (per slide)

```
[Hook — 1 sentence that earns the next 30 seconds]
[The point — what the slide says in plain speech]
[Evidence or story — 1 specific example]
[Bridge — what question this raises that the next slide answers]
```

## Anti-patterns for investor pitches

- ❌ "We're building the Uber for X" (lazy analogy, signals shallow thinking)
- ❌ "Our TAM is $400B" with no bottom-up reasoning
- ❌ Architecture diagram on slide 2 (investors fund markets and teams, not tech)
- ❌ NDA request before the meeting (signals inexperience)
- ❌ "We just need 0.1% of the market" (shows you don't know your GTM)
- ❌ Team slide before traction slide (lead with proof, not credentials)

## The 30-second test
Read only the action titles, top to bottom. If those 5 titles tell a complete story —
problem, solution, proof, why us, ask — the deck passes the basic structure test.
